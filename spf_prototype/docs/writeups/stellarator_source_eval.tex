\code{StellaratorSource}}

\author{SPF prototype -- methods evaluation memo}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We evaluate a proposed OpenMC neutron source, \code{StellaratorSource}, that samples
fusion birth positions directly from the true three-dimensional flux surfaces of a
solved stellarator equilibrium and birth directions from the spin-polarized
emission kernel about the local magnetic-field direction read from that same
equilibrium. The birth-position density is the metric measure
$p(s,\theta,\varphi)\propto S(s)\,\sg(s,\theta,\varphi)$, and we derive from first
principles the metric identity $\sg=R\,(\Rs\Zt-\Rt\Zs)$ (up to an
orientation-dependent sign) together with the fact that, unlike the axisymmetric
case, the toroidal angle $\varphi$ is \emph{not} uniform because $\sg$ depends on
$\varphi$. We show why Peterson's rejection-free closed-form (Bernstein-mixture)
tokamak sampler does not generalize---a stellarator $\sg$ is a device-specific,
non-separable two-dimensional Fourier density, not a finite polynomial in one
variable---and that what survives is the marginal$\to$conditional decomposition,
realized here as a rejection-free CDF cascade
$p(s)\,p(\varphi\mid s)\,p(\theta\mid s,\varphi)$ over a precomputed $\sg$ grid. We
prove unit birth weight and $O(\log N)$ sampling by construction, layer the
tier-1--validated Schwartz $P_2$ polarized-direction sampler on top with $\bhat$
taken from the \emph{same} grid, and lay out a computational-cost assessment and a
gated validation plan (V1--V5) with explicit acceptance thresholds. We are careful
throughout to separate what is proven analytically from what remains to be
confirmed numerically on a real DESC equilibrium, since the DESC producer is not
available on the current development machine.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem statement}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Existing sources mis-sample a stellarator plasma}

OpenMC's parametric fusion sources describe the plasma with an axisymmetric
tokamak parameterization. Peterson's native \code{TokamakSource} (PR \#3999)
is a substantial advance: it samples the plasma volume \emph{conformally} and
\emph{rejection-free} from the exact flux-coordinate density
$p(\tilde r,\alpha)\propto S(\tilde r)\,R(\tilde r,\alpha)\,\mathcal{J}(\tilde
r,\alpha)$ using a Miller flux-surface model
\begin{equation}
R(r,\alpha)=R_0+r\cos(\alpha+\delta\sin\alpha)+\Delta\!\left(1-(r/a)^2\right),
\qquad
Z(r,\alpha)=\kappa\, r\sin\alpha ,
\label{eq:miller}
\end{equation}
with $\varphi$ sampled uniformly. This is correct---but only because the tokamak
is axisymmetric. It cannot represent a stellarator's toroidally varying, shaped,
non-axisymmetric plasma.

Our own current prototype is worse in a specific, diagnosable way. The
\code{shape=plasma} branch of \code{polarized\_fusion\_source.cpp} samples a
\emph{circular} parabolic torus by \emph{rejection}:
\begin{equation}
p\sim R_0-a+2a\,\xi_1,\quad z\sim -a+2a\,\xi_2,\quad
\rho^2=\frac{(p-R_0)^2+z^2}{a^2},\quad
\text{accept if } \rho^2\le 1\ \wedge\ \xi_3\le\frac{(1-\rho^2)\,p}{p_{\max}} .
\label{eq:oldrej}
\end{equation}
Three coupled deficiencies follow, all of which the present design targets:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{No shaping.} The cross section is a circle of radius $a$: no
elongation $\kappa$, triangularity $\delta$, Shafranov shift $\Delta$, and---most
importantly for a stellarator---no $\varphi$-varying cross section at all.
\item \textbf{Rejection-based.} Acceptance in \eqref{eq:oldrej} is non-constant,
wastes RNG draws, and gives no \emph{closed-form} guarantee of unit birth weight:
the weight is unity only because we accept/reject rather than reweight, which is a
fragile coincidence rather than a proof.
\item \textbf{Position and field live on different geometries.} The real
equilibrium enters \emph{only} through $\bhat$. The birth point sits on a
\emph{circular} flux surface, while $\bhat$ is read from the \emph{actual}
stellarator field via a spatial $(R,\varphi,Z)\!\to\!\bhat$ KDTree/IDW lookup
(\code{bmode=fieldmap}, \code{spf\_fieldmap\_v1}). Two different flux-surface
objects are stitched together at a shared Cartesian point $(x,y,z)$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Requirement}

We require a source that (i) places births \emph{conformally} on the real 3D flux
surfaces of a solved equilibrium; (ii) carries \emph{unit} birth weight by
construction (no rejection, no importance reweighting); and (iii) reads the local
field direction $\bhat$ from the \emph{same} equilibrium at the \emph{same} point,
so position and field are self-consistent. This is the direct 3D generalization of
Peterson's tokamak result, and it also removes the position/field mismatch of the
current prototype. The polarized angular physics---already tier-1 validated---rides
on top unchanged, because position and direction remain exactly separable.

\section{The birth pdf and why the measure is $\sg$}
\label{sec:physics}

\subsection{Flux coordinates and the emission density}

Let $(s,\theta,\varphi)$ be flux coordinates: $s\in[0,1]$ a normalized flux label
(DESC $\rho=\sqrt{\psi_{\mathrm{tor}}/\psi_{\mathrm{tor,LCFS}}}$), $\theta$
poloidal, and $\varphi$ the toroidal angle, \emph{identified with the geometric
cylindrical angle} following the VMEC/DESC convention. The flux surfaces are
$R=R(s,\theta,\varphi)$, $Z=Z(s,\theta,\varphi)$, mapped to Cartesian by
\begin{equation}
x=R\cos\varphi,\qquad y=R\sin\varphi,\qquad z=Z .
\label{eq:cyl2cart}
\end{equation}
For a thermal DT plasma the fusion emission density is a \emph{flux function},
$S=S(s)$, because it is set by $n_i^2\langle\sigma v\rangle$ and both $n_i$ and
$T_i$ are constant on a flux surface to leading order. The physical (Cartesian)
volume element pulls back to
\begin{equation}
\dd V=\sg(s,\theta,\varphi)\dd s\dd\theta\dd\varphi ,
\label{eq:dV}
\end{equation}
where $\sg=\big|\det\partial(x,y,z)/\partial(s,\theta,\varphi)\big|$ is the metric
Jacobian. The number of births in a coordinate cell is $S(s)\dd V$, so the
\textbf{birth-position pdf} is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
p(s,\theta,\varphi)=\frac{S(s)\,\sg(s,\theta,\varphi)}{N},\qquad
N=\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{2\pi}
S(s)\,\sg(s,\theta,\varphi)\dd\varphi\dd\theta\dd s\; }
\label{eq:pdf}
\end{equation}
The measure is $\sg$, not $\dd s\dd\theta\dd\varphi$, precisely because a uniform
draw in flux coordinates over-weights regions where the coordinate cells are small
in physical volume; \eqref{eq:dV} is the change of variables that corrects this.

\subsection{The metric, derived}
\label{sec:metric}

Write $\es=\partial_s(x,y,z)$, $\et=\partial_\theta(x,y,z)$,
$\ep=\partial_\varphi(x,y,z)$ for the columns of the Jacobian, and $R_s\equiv
\partial R/\partial s$, etc. Differentiating \eqref{eq:cyl2cart},
\begin{align}
\es&=(\Rs\cos\varphi,\ \Rs\sin\varphi,\ \Zs),\\
\et&=(\Rt\cos\varphi,\ \Rt\sin\varphi,\ \Zt),\\
\ep&=(\Rp\cos\varphi-R\sin\varphi,\ \Rp\sin\varphi+R\cos\varphi,\ \Zp).
\end{align}
The signed Jacobian is the triple product $\det=\es\cdot(\et\times\ep)$. The cross
product is, component by component,
\begin{align}
(\et\times\ep)_x&=\Zp\Rt\sin\varphi-\Zt\Rp\sin\varphi-\Zt R\cos\varphi,\\
(\et\times\ep)_y&=\Zt\Rp\cos\varphi-\Zt R\sin\varphi-\Rt\Zp\cos\varphi,\\
(\et\times\ep)_z&=\Rt R\,(\cos^2\varphi+\sin^2\varphi)=R\,\Rt .
\end{align}
The $z$-component already shows the key collapse: the $\Rp$ terms cancel and the
$k$-component reduces to $R\,\Rt$. Dotting with $\es$, \emph{every}
$\sin\varphi\cos\varphi$ term---and these are exactly the ones carrying the
$\varphi$-derivatives $\Rp,\Zp$---cancels in pairs, and the $\cos^2\varphi$ and
$\sin^2\varphi$ terms combine, leaving
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\det\partial(x,y,z)/\partial(s,\theta,\varphi)
=R\,(\Rt\Zs-\Rs\Zt)
=-\,R\,\Jpol,\qquad
\Jpol\equiv \Rs\Zt-\Rt\Zs=\frac{\partial(R,Z)}{\partial(s,\theta)}\; }
\label{eq:metric}
\end{equation}
(verified symbolically). Two remarks matter for correctness:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The $\varphi$-\emph{derivatives} drop, but the
$\varphi$-\emph{dependence} does not.} No $\Rp$ or $\Zp$ survives---this is why the
result has the same \emph{form} as the tokamak metric, ``major radius $R$ times a
poloidal Jacobian.'' But $R(s,\theta,\varphi)$, $\Rs$, $\Rt$, $\Zs$, $\Zt$ each
still carry $\varphi$, because the cross section breathes with toroidal angle.
Hence $\sg=\sg(s,\theta,\varphi)$.
\item \textbf{Sign / orientation.} The physical measure in \eqref{eq:dV} uses the
\emph{magnitude}, $\sg=|R\,\Jpol|=R\,|\Rs\Zt-\Rt\Zs|$ (with $R>0$). The overall
sign of \eqref{eq:metric} depends on the handedness of the $(s,\theta,\varphi)$
ordering and on DESC's internal convention; our derivation with ordering
$(\es,\et,\ep)$ yields $-R\,\Jpol$, whereas the design memo boxes the opposite-sign
form $R\,(\Rs\Zt-\Rt\Zs)$. Only $|\sg|$ enters the pdf, so this is immaterial to
sampling \emph{provided} $\sg$ is single-signed over the interior---which is exactly
what validation gate V2 and convention flag \code{[C5]} enforce.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why $\varphi$ is not uniform, and the contrast with Peterson}
\label{sec:notuniform}

\paragraph{Axisymmetric (tokamak) limit.} If $R=R(s,\theta)$, $Z=Z(s,\theta)$ with
no $\varphi$-dependence, then $\sg=R(s,\theta)\,|\Jpol(s,\theta)|$ is independent of
$\varphi$ and \eqref{eq:pdf} \emph{factorizes}:
\begin{equation}
p(s,\theta,\varphi)=
\underbrace{\frac{S(s)\,R(s,\theta)\,|\Jpol(s,\theta)|}{N}}_{p(s,\theta)}
\;\cdot\;
\underbrace{\frac{1}{2\pi}}_{p(\varphi)} .
\label{eq:tokamakfactor}
\end{equation}
This is precisely Peterson's $p(\tilde r,\alpha)\propto S(\tilde r)\,R\,\mathcal{J}$
with $\varphi\sim U[0,2\pi)$. The $R$ factor is the ``toroidal effect'': differential
volume is larger at larger $R$, and because $(s,\theta)$ (his $(\tilde r,\alpha)$)
correlate with $R$, the two flux coordinates must be sampled \emph{jointly}, not
independently.

\paragraph{Stellarator.} Here $\sg=R(s,\theta,\varphi)\,|\Jpol(s,\theta,\varphi)|$
\emph{depends on} $\varphi$, with two consequences:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$\varphi$ is not uniform.} Its marginal is
$p(\varphi)\propto\iint S(s)\,\sg(s,\theta,\varphi)\dd s\dd\theta$, which varies with
$\varphi$: more births occur where the local flux tube is fatter or shifted to
larger $R$. Sampling $\varphi$ uniformly---the fatal shortcut---would systematically
misplace births toroidally.
\item \textbf{$(s,\theta,\varphi)$ are jointly correlated.} No factorization
survives; the full 3D density must be sampled respecting the coupling. This is the
direct generalization of Peterson's $(\tilde r,\alpha)$ correlation to one more
dimension.
\end{itemize}
The single fact ``$\sg$ depends on $\varphi$'' is the entire reason a stellarator
source needs more than ``tokamak pattern $+$ uniform $\varphi$.''

\section{Why Peterson's closed form does not generalize}
\label{sec:bernstein}

Peterson's construction is rejection-free \emph{and} closed-form: he expands the
joint weight $S(\tilde r)\,R\,\mathcal{J}$ into a finite Bernstein-polynomial
mixture and samples each term analytically. That closure rests on three
axisymmetry-specific properties:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $R(\tilde r,\alpha)$ and $Z(\tilde r,\alpha)$ from \eqref{eq:miller} are
\emph{low-degree} polynomial/trig-polynomial parametric surfaces;
\item hence $\mathcal{J}(\tilde r,\alpha)$ and the product
$S(\tilde r)\,R\,\mathcal{J}$ are \emph{finite polynomials} in $\tilde r$ (and finite
trigonometric polynomials in $\alpha$)---exactly a Bernstein basis of modest degree;
\item there is \emph{no} $\varphi$-dependence, so the closed form is 2D in $(\tilde
r,\alpha)$ and $\varphi$ is free.
\end{enumerate}

For a stellarator, $\sg$ obtained from a VMEC/DESC equilibrium is a
\emph{truncated Fourier series}
\begin{equation}
\sg(s,\theta,\varphi)=\sum_{m,n} g_{mn}(s)\,
\cos\!\big(m\theta-n\Nfp\varphi\big)\;(+\ \text{sine terms if not
stellarator-symmetric}),
\label{eq:fouriersg}
\end{equation}
with device-specific harmonics $g_{mn}(s)$ and a genuine two-dimensional
$(\theta,\varphi)$ structure that is \emph{not} a finite polynomial in a single
variable and is \emph{not separable}. There is no exact finite Bernstein mixture for
a general non-separable 2D Fourier density, so the closed-form, term-by-term sampler
does not carry over.

What \emph{does} carry over is the \emph{strategy}, not the closed form:
\begin{itemize}
\item the \textbf{marginal$\to$conditional decomposition} $p(s)$ then
$p(\theta,\varphi\mid s)$;
\item \textbf{rejection-free, unit-weight} sampling by inverse-CDF;
\end{itemize}
now realized with a \emph{precomputed numerical CDF cascade} built from the
equilibrium's own $\sg$ grid, in place of an analytic Bernstein mixture. This is the
algorithm of \S\ref{sec:algorithm}.

\section{The algorithm: a rejection-free CDF cascade}
\label{sec:algorithm}

\subsection{Inputs and construction (once, then \code{const})}

On the DESC flux grid $s_i\ (i=0..N_s-1)$, $\theta_j\ (j=0..N_\theta-1)$,
$\varphi_k\ (k=0..N_\varphi-1)$ over the \emph{full} torus, the producer supplies the
samples $g_{ijk}=|\sg(s_i,\theta_j,\varphi_k)|\ge0$, the geometry $R_{ijk},Z_{ijk}$,
the field direction $\bhat_{ijk}$ (\S\ref{sec:field}), quadrature weights
$\Delta s_i,\Delta\theta_j,\Delta\varphi_k$, and the emission profile $S(s_i)$. In the
constructor we form the discretized joint pdf (unnormalized node weights)
\begin{equation}
w_{ijk}=S(s_i)\,g_{ijk}\,\Delta s_i\,\Delta\theta_j\,\Delta\varphi_k ,
\label{eq:wijk}
\end{equation}
and three cumulative tables realizing the chain rule
$p(s,\theta,\varphi)=p(s)\,p(\varphi\mid s)\,p(\theta\mid s,\varphi)$:
\begin{align}
&\textbf{Marginal in }s:& W_i&=\sum_{j,k}w_{ijk},&
&C^{(s)}_i=\frac{\sum_{i'\le i}W_{i'}}{\sum_{i'}W_{i'}} ;\\
&\textbf{Conditional }\varphi\mid s:& W^{(i)}_k&=\sum_{j}w_{ijk},&
&C^{(\varphi\mid i)}_k=\frac{\sum_{k'\le k}W^{(i)}_{k'}}{\sum_{k'}W^{(i)}_{k'}} ;\\
&\textbf{Conditional }\theta\mid(s,\varphi):& &&
&C^{(\theta\mid i,k)}_j=\frac{\sum_{j'\le j}w_{ij'k}}{\sum_{j'}w_{ij'k}} .
\end{align}
Note $\sum_{j,k} g_{ijk}\Delta\theta_j\Delta\varphi_k\approx \dd V/\dd s$, the
flux-shell volume, so $W_i\propto S(s_i)\,(\dd V/\dd s)|_{s_i}$---the physically
correct radial emission profile. The order $s\!\to\!\varphi\!\to\!\theta$ is chosen so
the innermost conditional is over $\theta$, where $\sg$ varies most smoothly; the
$\varphi\leftrightarrow\theta$ order is otherwise free.

\subsection{Sampling (\code{sample(seed) const}), rejection-free, $\mathrm{wgt}=1$}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Draw $u_1=\code{prn}$; binary-search $C^{(s)}$ for cell $i^\ast$; interpolate
$s$ inside $[s_{i^\ast},s_{i^\ast+1}]$ by inverting the local linear CDF ramp.
\item Draw $u_2$; binary-search $C^{(\varphi\mid i^\ast)}$ $\to$ cell $k^\ast$;
interpolate $\varphi$.
\item Draw $u_3$; binary-search $C^{(\theta\mid i^\ast,k^\ast)}$ $\to$ cell $j^\ast$;
interpolate $\theta$.
\item Map $(s,\theta,\varphi)\to(R,Z)$ by trilinear interpolation of stored
$R_{ijk},Z_{ijk}$ (or by evaluating the interior Fourier modes), then
$x=R\cos\varphi,\ y=R\sin\varphi,\ z=Z$.
\item Read $\bhat$ at the \emph{same} $(s,\theta,\varphi)$ (\S\ref{sec:field});
assert $\big||\bhat|-1\big|<10^{-12}$.
\item Emit the \code{SourceSite} with $\mathrm{wgt}=1$, $\mathrm{particle}=$ neutron.
\end{enumerate}
Each sample costs $O(\log N_s+\log N_\varphi+\log N_\theta)$.

\subsection{Unit-birth-weight and unbiasedness --- proof}
\label{sec:unitweight}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Claim.} Every emitted site has $\mathrm{wgt}=1$ exactly, and the ensemble of
sampled cells $(i^\ast,j^\ast,k^\ast)$ is an unbiased draw from the discrete target
$p_{ijk}=w_{ijk}/\sum w$.
\end{quote}
\emph{Proof.} The cascade draws the cell with probability
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{\frac{W_{i^\ast}}{\sum_i W_i}}_{p(i^\ast)}\cdot
\underbrace{\frac{W^{(i^\ast)}_{k^\ast}}{\sum_k W^{(i^\ast)}_k}}_{p(k^\ast\mid
i^\ast)}\cdot
\underbrace{\frac{w_{i^\ast j^\ast k^\ast}}{\sum_j w_{i^\ast j k^\ast}}}_{p(j^\ast\mid
i^\ast,k^\ast)} .
\end{equation}
Substituting $W_{i}=\sum_{jk}w_{ijk}$ and $W^{(i)}_k=\sum_j w_{ijk}$, the
denominators telescope: $\sum_k W^{(i^\ast)}_k=W_{i^\ast}$ and $\sum_j w_{i^\ast j
k^\ast}=W^{(i^\ast)}_{k^\ast}$, so the product collapses to
\begin{equation}
\frac{w_{i^\ast j^\ast k^\ast}}{\sum_i W_i}=\frac{w_{i^\ast j^\ast
k^\ast}}{\sum_{ijk} w_{ijk}}=p_{i^\ast j^\ast k^\ast}.
\end{equation}
The cell is thus drawn with exactly its target mass. No proposal is ever rejected and
no importance ratio is applied, so the assigned weight is the constant $1$. The
within-cell inverse-CDF interpolation refines the continuous $(s,\theta,\varphi)$ while
preserving each cell's probability mass, leaving the marginal cell probabilities
exact. Hence $\mathrm{wgt}\equiv1$ and unbiasedness \emph{to the discretized pdf} hold
\emph{by construction}; the sole approximation is $p_{ijk}$ versus the continuous $p$,
a deterministic grid error that $\to0$ under refinement. $\qquad\blacksquare$

This matches Peterson's guarantee and the existing sampler's invariant, but---unlike
\eqref{eq:oldrej}---it is a proof, not a coincidence of accept/reject.

\subsection{Independent oracle: the rejection fallback}

For cross-checking only, a simpler sampler draws $s$ from the same 1D marginal
$p(s)$, proposes $\theta,\varphi\sim U[0,2\pi)^2$, and accepts with probability
$\sg(s,\theta,\varphi)/g_{\max}(s)$, $g_{\max}(s)=\max_{\theta,\varphi}\sg$; accepted
samples carry $\mathrm{wgt}=1$. Its efficiency
$\langle\sg\rangle(s)/\big((2\pi)^2 g_{\max}(s)\big)$ degrades for peaked $\sg$
(strong shaping, near-axis), so it is a bring-up/QA oracle, not the target. A
weighted variant (propose uniformly, $\mathrm{wgt}\propto\sg$) \emph{violates} the
unit-weight requirement and is explicitly not used. The cascade (a) and rejection (b)
samplers must produce statistically identical $(s,\theta,\varphi)$ distributions
(KS/$\chi^2$)---the inverse-CDF-vs-rejection cross-check already trusted in tier 1.

\section{The spin-polarized direction sampler, layered on top}
\label{sec:spf}

\subsection{Field from the same equilibrium}
\label{sec:field}

Position and field must be self-consistent. DESC computes $B$ on \emph{exactly} the
$(\rho,\theta,\zeta)$ grid used for $\sg$ (\code{eq.compute(["R","phi","Z","B"],\dots)}),
so we store $\bhat_{ijk}=(B/|B|)_{ijk}$ as a Cartesian unit vector (via the existing
$B_{\mathrm{cyl}}\!\to\!$Cartesian rotation in the producer) in the flux map alongside
$\sg,R,Z$. In \code{sample()}, after drawing $(s,\theta,\varphi)$, we read $\bhat$ at
the \emph{same} flux node (trilinear interpolation on $(s,\theta,\varphi)$,
renormalized): no KDTree, no circular-vs-DESC mismatch. This is the concrete fix for
deficiency (3) of \S\ref{sec:problem}.

\subsection{The polarized emission kernel and its separation}

The Schwartz $P_2$ kernel gives the neutron differential cross section as a
quadrupolar (rank-2) anisotropy about $\bhat$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\dd\sigma}{\dd\Omega}=\frac{\sigma_0}{2\pi}\left[
\tfrac34\,a\,\sin^2\theta+\Big(\tfrac23 b+\tfrac13 c\Big)\Big(\tfrac14+\tfrac34\cos^2\theta\Big)\right],
\qquad\theta=\angle(\uhat,\bhat).
\label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
Its two angular shapes share only two effective weights,
\begin{equation}
w_\perp=\tfrac34 a\quad(\sin^2\theta\text{ shape}),\qquad
w_\parallel=\tfrac23 b+\tfrac13 c\quad(\tfrac14+\tfrac34\cos^2\theta\text{ shape}),
\end{equation}
and, using $\int\sin^2\theta\dd\Omega=8\pi/3$ and
$\int(\tfrac14+\tfrac34\cos^2\theta)\dd\Omega=2\pi$, the stratified selection
probability of the perpendicular shape is
\begin{equation}
P_\perp=\frac{W_\perp}{W_\perp+W_\parallel}=\frac{a}{\eta},\qquad
\eta=a+\tfrac23 b+\tfrac13 c=\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}}{\sigma_0}.
\label{eq:eta}
\end{equation}
\textbf{The direction and the rate are separate concerns.} The two weights
$(w_\perp,w_\parallel)$ fully determine the \emph{birth direction} for any $(a,b,c)$;
the scalar rate factor $\eta$ scales source \emph{strength} independently. In
particular $B$ and $C$ share the same angular shape and differ only in $\eta$
(corners: $\eta=\tfrac23,1,\tfrac23,\tfrac13$ for non-pol, $A$, $B$, $C$), so
isotropic and pure-$C$ have identical \emph{normalized} directionality. This
separation is unchanged from the tier-1--validated sampler; the conformal position
sampler simply feeds it a self-consistent $\bhat$.

\subsection{Sampling and rotation}

$\cos\theta$ about $\bhat$ is drawn by stratified selection of the two shapes, each by
a closed-form inverse-CDF (the depressed-cubic roots of $x^3-3x+(4u-2)=0$ for
$\sin^2\theta$ and $x^3+x+(2-4u)=0$ for $\tfrac14+\tfrac34\cos^2\theta$), with a
rejection cross-check; $\varphi\sim U[0,2\pi)$. The local direction is rotated to
global with a Gram--Schmidt frame built from the \emph{least-aligned} reference axis
(\code{argmin}$_e|\bhat\cdot\hat e|$), which avoids the $\bhat\parallel\zhat$
degeneracy. All of this is the existing \code{spf::sample\_global\_direction}; the
only new coupling is that $\bhat$ now comes from the same equilibrium grid as the
position, closing the position/field loop.

\section{Computational evaluation}
\label{sec:compute}

\subsection{DESC solve cost and the separate-venv dependency}

The equilibrium is produced \emph{offline}. \code{quasr\_equilibrium\_field.py} builds
a fixed-boundary DESC \code{Equilibrium} from a QUASR device boundary
(\code{FourierRZToroidalSurface}, resolution $L{=}M{=}N{=}6$) and solves it
(\code{eq.solve(ftol=1e-2, maxiter=50)}). The dominant cost is this JAX-accelerated
pseudospectral Newton solve; it is a \emph{one-time} cost per device, amortized over
every Monte Carlo history, and does not touch the transport hot loop. The
\code{StellaratorSource} evaluation \emph{extends} this producer minimally: add
\code{"sqrt(g)"} to the existing \code{eq.compute([...])} list (it is returned on the
same \code{LinearGrid} for free) and write a new portable map \code{spf\_fluxmap\_v1}
carrying $\sg,R,Z,\bhat$ on the $(s,\theta,\varphi)$ grid plus the axes, $\Nfp$, and
the $S(s)$ profile spec.

\textbf{Dependency boundary.} DESC's jax/numpy pins conflict with OpenMC and DESC is
unavailable on the aarch64/osx development box, so the map is produced in an isolated
\code{desc\_venv} on a login node; the transport/C++ consumer stays DESC-free and reads
only the portable map. This is the same producer/consumer split already used for
\code{spf\_fieldmap\_v1}.

\subsection{Grid resolution versus sampling bias}

The \emph{only} approximation in the cascade (\S\ref{sec:unitweight}) is the finite
resolution of $\sg$ on the grid. This bias is \textbf{deterministic and diagnosable by
refinement}, not stochastic: it does not appear as variance and cannot be hidden by
running more histories. Under-resolution distorts the birth profile where $\sg$ is
peaked---strongly shaped surfaces and near-axis regions. Mitigation is to refine
$N_s,N_\theta,N_\varphi$ (storage is cheap, \S\ref{sec:algorithm}) or to store a
spline / higher-order representation of $\sg$. The recommended default follows the
producer's $12\times48\times144$ grid with $N_s\gtrsim32$ for the marginal;
convergence is confirmed by gates V1 and V4 under refinement.

\subsection{Near-axis handling}

As $s\to0$ the coordinates collapse to the magnetic axis curve and $\sg\to0$. The
$s$-marginal is well-behaved because $\sg\to0$ kills the weight, but (i) the
$(\theta,\varphi)$ conditional is ill-defined at $s=0$ (all $\theta$ map to one point)
and (ii) $R,Z,\bhat$ interpolation is singular there. The producer already starts the
grid at $s_{\min}=0.05$; the innermost shell is treated as a line source or via a
near-axis expansion, and the vanishing weight there means the excluded core
contributes negligibly to $N$. Gate V2 (single-signed $\sg$ for $s>0$) is stated for
$s>0$ precisely to exclude the axis.

\subsection{Honest scope and limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$S=S(s)$ flux-function assumption.} $p\propto S(s)\sg$ assumes emission
depends on $s$ only. Real in-surface $T_i/n_i$ asymmetries would break it (out of
scope; noted). The \emph{angular} physics is unaffected---position and direction
remain exactly separable, and $\eta(a,b,c)$ still scales strength independently.
\item \textbf{Producer-side DESC dependency.} The map is offline-only; convention
hazards \code{[C1]--[C3]} (boundary$\to$DESC mode signs/$\Nfp$, solve convergence,
$B\!\cdot\!\hat n\!\approx\!0$ on the LCFS) plus the new \code{[C4]} (flux-label
definition matching $S(s)$) and \code{[C5]} ($\sg$ sign) must be checked on the DESC
machine.
\item \textbf{Interpolation fidelity.} Trilinear interpolation of stored $R,Z$
(matching the fieldmap approach) is the recommended default; evaluating interior
Fourier modes in-consumer is higher fidelity but re-introduces a mode-convention
surface---a documented upgrade, not the first step.
\item \textbf{Deterministic grid bias.} Not a variance; refine to remove (V1, V4).
\item \textbf{Angular physics is birth-only.} No depolarization in transport, energy
stays decoupled (monoenergetic / Ballabio), no alpha channel, convex-wall /
free-stream NWL validation only---matching the prototype's and Schwartz's own
caveats.
\end{itemize}

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:summary}

The tokamak result $p\propto S\,R\,\mathcal{J}$ is the axisymmetric shadow of the
general $p\propto S\,\sg$; the stellarator merely refuses to let $\sg$---and therefore
$\varphi$---be uniform. We derived the metric identity
$\sg=R\,|\Rs\Zt-\Rt\Zs|$ and its $\varphi$-derivative cancellation, showed why
Peterson's Bernstein closed form does not survive (a device-specific, non-separable 2D
Fourier density is not a finite polynomial), and established that the surviving
strategy---a rejection-free marginal$\to$conditional CDF cascade over the
equilibrium's own $\sg$ grid---samples the exact discretized density with proven unit
birth weight in $O(\log N)$. Layering the tier-1--validated $P_2$ direction sampler on
top, with $\bhat$ read from the \emph{same} grid, yields a single, self-consistent,
equilibrium-conformal, spin-polarized source that fixes the circular-torus fake, the
rejection weight, the position/field mismatch, and the non-periodic Cartesian
workaround at once. What is proven here is the measure and the sampler's exactness;
what remains is to run the DESC producer and clear gates V1--V5 on a real equilibrium.
