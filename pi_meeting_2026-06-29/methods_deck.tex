% ---- A1: quadrature & the hybrid ----
\begin{frame}{A1. Quadrature, and my hybrid quadrature}
  \small
  \textbf{Quadrature} = approximating a definite integral by a weighted sum at chosen nodes,
  $\int f \approx \sum_i w_i f(\phi_i)$ (here Gauss--Legendre in the toroidal angle $\phi$).
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Two ways to do the per-loop $\phi$-integral} $g=\int \mathcal{J}\mathcal{N}\,K/D^{3/2}\dd\phi$:
      (i) reduce it to closed form (elliptic $F,E$ + elementary) via the series; (ii) just
      evaluate it numerically.
    \item \textbf{My hybrid hybridizes the two:} keep the integrand
      \emph{exact and analytic} (no $\eps$-truncation) --- $\mathcal{J},\mathcal{N},D,\Bhat$ as explicit
      differentiable functions of the design parameters --- but do the single residual $\phi$-quadrature
      \emph{numerically}.
    \item \textbf{Why:} it has \emph{no $\eps_{\mathrm{eff}}$ ceiling} below grazing and, unlike a
      black-box numerical NWL, \emph{retains exact autodiff gradients} in all design parameters.
      This is Lion's cost regime but differentiable.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---- A2: low-eps_eff QA adaptation ----
\begin{frame}{A2. What I did to Schwartz's method for QA plasmas}
  \small
  Schwartz (2025) is \emph{exact} for axisymmetric rings: a planar circle makes
  $D_0(\phi)=p^2+r^2+z^2-2pr\cos\phi$ affine in $\cos\phi$, so $w=e^{i\phi}$ puts the integral on a
  genus-1 (elliptic) curve $\Rightarrow$ closed form in $F,E$.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item A stellarator loop is non-planar; the period integral becomes \textbf{hyperelliptic}
      (genus $\ge 2$) --- no finite closed form in $F,E$.
    \item \textbf{My addition:} a \emph{field-period perturbation series}. Split each loop into mean
      $+\,\eps\,$ripple, expand $D^{-s}$, and every term lands in the canonical form
      (trig polynomial)$/D_0^{(2j+1)/2}$ --- reducible to the \emph{same} $F,E$.
    \item Leading order $g^{(0)}$ \emph{is} Schwartz's kernel verbatim; corrections are additive
      ripple terms layered on the existing Carlson-form code. Reduces \emph{exactly} to the
      axisymmetric kernel at zero ripple (regression test, $2\times10^{-16}$).
    \item Controlling parameter is the \emph{geometric} non-axisymmetry $\eps_{\mathrm{eff}}$, not
      $|B|$ quasisymmetry --- QA (gently shaped) is where the truncated series converges fastest.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{A3. The spin-polarized DT source in OpenMC}
  \small
  A self-contained \texttt{CompiledSource} (\texttt{src/polarized\_fusion\_source.cpp},
  \texttt{src/spf\_sampler.hpp}), no private MIT code.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Anisotropic emission:} monoenergetic 14.1\,MeV; birth direction sampled from
      $I(\theta)=\tfrac34 a\sin^2\theta+(\tfrac23 b+\tfrac13 c)(\tfrac14+\tfrac34\cos^2\theta)$ by
      \emph{stratified} sampling of the two angular shapes (exact inverse-CDF, cross-checked vs
      rejection). Rate factor $\eta=a+\tfrac23 b+\tfrac13 c$ kept \emph{separate} from the
      direction PDF.
    \item \textbf{Quantization axis = local field:} $\cos\theta=u\cdot\Bhat(\rs)$ with $\Bhat$ from
      \texttt{field\_->bhat()}; sample in a $\Bhat$-aligned frame, rotate to global with a
      Gram--Schmidt basis (no gimbal degeneracy).
    \item \textbf{Plugin loading:} OpenMC loads the compiled \texttt{.so} via
      \texttt{CompiledSource(library=...)}; thread-safe ($\Kpol$ state set at construction, all
      sampling \texttt{const}, RNG only \texttt{openmc::prn(seed)}).
    \item Verified: A/iso $=\tfrac32\sin^2\theta$ to 0.3\%/bin, mean $1.0000$; B$\equiv$C shape;
      linearity of mixes.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---- A4: analytic improvements / free-streaming work ----
\begin{frame}{A4. Analytic work in the free-streaming limit}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Reduction engine:} harmonic recursion $\Ck{k+1}{s}=2h\Ck{k}{s}-\Ck{k-1}{s}-2\Ck{k}{s-1}$,
      master recursion for $\Ja{a}=\int(h-\cos\phi)^a\dd\phi$, $F/E$ seeds through the existing
      Carlson $R_F/R_D$ layer. \emph{No third-kind ($\Pi$) integral ever appears.}
    \item \textbf{Polarized kernels:} A $=\,$iso$-\,$cos$^2$, B/C $=\tfrac14$iso$+\tfrac34$cos$^2$;
      one extra power $1/D$ raises the elliptic order ($s$: $5/2\!\to\!7/2\!\to\!9/2$). Order-by-order
      cross-check $g_A^{(j)}+g_c^{(j)}=g_{\mathrm{iso}}^{(j)}$ from $\sin^2+\cos^2=1$.
    \item \textbf{Gradient closure:} $\partial_h\Ck{k}{s}=-s\,\Ck{k}{s+1}$ --- differentiation maps
      the integral family into itself, so optimization gradients cost \emph{no} new special-function
      calls and give a second, independent generator of the elliptic tower.
    \item \textbf{Robustness:} singularity subtraction for grazing; Leibniz boundary term for the
      moving visible arc; multi-arc visibility ($\mathcal{N}=\nhat\cdot\Del>0$).
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---- A5: elliptic integrals ----
\begin{frame}{A5. Elliptic integrals, and where they enter}
  \small
  Incomplete elliptic integrals of the first / second kind ($m=k^2$):
  \begin{equation*}
    \Fell(\varphi,m)=\int_0^{\varphi}\!\frac{\dd x}{\sqrt{1-m\sin^2 x}},\qquad
    \Eell(\varphi,m)=\int_0^{\varphi}\!\sqrt{1-m\sin^2 x}\,\dd x
  \end{equation*}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item They are the closed form for $\int (h-\cos\phi)^{-1/2}\dd\phi$ and
      $\int(h-\cos\phi)^{+1/2}\dd\phi$ --- the half-integer-power integrals the flux factor
      ($D^{-3/2}$) and polarization kernels ($D^{-5/2}$) produce.
    \item Seeds: $\Ja{-1/2}\propto \Fell(\phi_\pm/2,m)$, $\Ja{+1/2}\propto \Eell(\phi_\pm/2,m)$,
      modulus $m=-2/(h-1)=-4pr/((p-r)^2+z^2)$, $h=(p^2+r^2+z^2)/2pr$.
    \item Implemented through \textbf{Carlson symmetric forms} $R_F,R_D$ --- numerically stable,
      differentiable, and they handle the \emph{asymmetric} visible arcs (evaluate at $\phi_+/2$ and
      $\phi_-/2$, difference) without modification.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---- A6: perturbation expansion setup ----
\begin{frame}{A6. How the perturbation expansion was set up}
  \small
  Split loop into mean $+$ ripple (formal order parameter $\eps$):
  \begin{equation*}
    R(\phi)=p+\eps\,dR(\phi),\qquad Z(\phi)-z_w=z+\eps\,dZ(\phi)
  \end{equation*}
  $dR,dZ$ expanded in field-period harmonics $\cos/\sin(n\Nfp\phi)$. Then \emph{exactly}:
  \begin{equation*}
    D=D_0+\eps D_1+\eps^2 D_2,\quad
    D_1=2[dR(p-r\cos\phi)+z\,dZ],\quad D_2=dR^2+dZ^2
  \end{equation*}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Jacobian $\mathcal{J}$, flux numerator $\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{N}_0+\eps\mathcal{N}_1$, and
      field projection $\Bhat\cdot\Del=P_0+\eps P_1+\eps^2 P_2$ expand the same way.
    \item Insert into $g$, use $D^{-s}=D_0^{-s}(1+\eps D_1/D_0+\eps^2 D_2/D_0)^{-s}$, collect powers
      of $\eps$ $\Rightarrow$ strict series $g=g^{(0)}+\eps g^{(1)}+\eps^2 g^{(2)}+\cdots$.
    \item \textbf{Implementation:} represent $dR,dZ,\mathcal{N},P,D_1,D_2,\ldots$ as Fourier
      coefficient vectors; form products by \emph{convolution}; contract against
      $\Ck{k}{s},\Sk{k}{s}$. General; avoids hand-derived coefficients.
    \item Verified $g^{(1)},g^{(2)}$ vs finite differences of the exact integral to 9--11 sig figs
      at \emph{asymmetric} limits.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---- A6b: the subtle 2nd-order polarized term ----
\begin{frame}{A6b. The subtle second-order polarized term}
  \small
  Naive expansion $\Bhat\approx\Bhat_0+\eps\bperp$ gives $P_2=\bperp\cdot\Del_1$. But
  $|\Bhat|=1$ is only enforced to \emph{first} order by $\Bhat_0\cdot\bperp=0$.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item At second order the unit-norm constraint demands a field term $\eps^2\bperp_2$ with
      $\Bhat_0\cdot\bperp_2=-\tfrac12|\bperp|^2$, contributing $\bperp_2\cdot\Del_0$ to $P_2$.
    \item This is the second-order Taylor remainder of the angled-field numerator $P_0$ in the
      field-angle excursions $\alpha_1,\beta_1$ (closed-form trig polynomials).
    \item \textbf{Why it matters:} omit it and the A, B/C modes converge only at \emph{second}
      order; include it and they recover the \emph{third}-order convergence of the isotropic series.
      Small for QA (small $|\bperp|$), but it is the correct closed form. Verified to 9 sig figs.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---- A7: hybrid solution combining analytic + numerical ----
\begin{frame}{A7. What the hybrid solution \emph{is}}
  \small
  Three regimes, separated by two thresholds; the hybrid bridges them:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $\eps_{\mathrm{eff}}\lesssim\tfrac12$: \textbf{truncated series} --- fast, closed form,
      differentiable (2 terms $\to$1\% for $\eps_{\mathrm{eff}}\lesssim0.15$; 3 terms $\to\approx0.3$).
    \item $\tfrac12\lesssim\eps_{\mathrm{eff}}\lesssim\eps_{\mathrm{graze}}\!\approx\!2$:
      \textbf{hybrid quadrature} --- exact integrand, single numerical $\phi$-integral, exact
      gradients retained. Reproduces ground truth to $\sim2\times10^{-9}$.
    \item Near grazing ($\min_\phi|\Del|\!\to\!0$): \textbf{singularity-subtracted hybrid} ---
      closed-form peak model removed, fixed node count suffices ($\sim$12 nodes).
    \item True contact / scattered load: \textbf{Monte Carlo} required.
  \end{itemize}
  The series is the \emph{analytic backbone} that makes every stage differentiable; the hybrid is
  the same integrand, not a different model.
\end{frame}

% ---- A8: other core concepts ----
\begin{frame}{A8. Other concepts the work leans on}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Boozer coordinates / quasisymmetry:} QA/QH are statements that $|B|$ depends on
      Boozer angles only through a fixed combination --- they govern \emph{confinement}, not directly
      this expansion.
    \item \textbf{$\eps_{\mathrm{eff}}$ vs qs-error:} my controlling parameter is
      $\eps_{\mathrm{eff}}\sim\max_\phi\sqrt{dR^2+dZ^2}/\min_\phi|\Del_0|$ --- \emph{real-space} boundary
      shaping over standoff. The quasisymmetry error (deviation of $|B|$ from exact QS) is a
      \emph{different} number; a QA config can be excellent in $|B|$ yet strongly shaped geometrically.
    \item \textbf{TBR:} tritium breeding ratio --- needs a breeder blanket + scattering; the
      free-streaming inboard/outboard current split is a geometric \emph{precursor}, not a TBR.
    \item \textbf{Spin-state fractions $(a,b,c)$:} from deuteron/triton spin projections;
      $a{+}b{+}c{=}1$; nonpolarized $=(\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac13)$ gives $\sigma=\tfrac23\sigma_0$.
      B and C share angular shape (differ only in rate).
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---- B1 derivation & validity ----
\begin{frame}{B1. Is the derivation right, and physically valid?}
  \small
  \textbf{Derivation.} Every piece is verified numerically: seeds $\Ja{\pm1/2}$ ($2\times10^{-15}$),
  master recursion ($6\times10^{-14}$), harmonic recursion ($9\times10^{-12}$), $h$-derivative
  closures vs autodiff ($\sim10^{-14}$), $g^{(0)},g_c^{(0)}$ vs exact quadrature ($\sim4\times10^{-16}$),
  $g^{(1)},g^{(2)},g_c^{(1)},g_c^{(2)}$ vs finite differences (limited only by step). The Appendix-C
  worked single-harmonic example reproduces exactly.\\[0.4em]
  \textbf{Validity.} It is the \emph{free-streaming / first-flight} (uncollided) wall load: no
  scattering, no multiplication. That is a genuine physical limit (the uncollided tally of any
  transport code), \emph{and} it is exactly why it is a clean ground truth. The assumption is ideal
  field-aligned polarization; a fraction $<1$ interpolates toward isotropic and leaves the geometry
  untouched.
\end{frame}

% ---- B2 convergence ----
\begin{frame}{B2. Convergence and error control}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Truncation error at order $p$ scales as $\eps_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\,p+1}$; fitted log--log
      slopes $1.00, 2.03, 3.01$ (iso) and $\approx$ same for A, B/C.
    \item Practical table (representative loop): $\eps_{\mathrm{eff}}=0.10\!\to\!2.9\%/0.12\%/0.024\%$;
      $0.30\!\to\!7.1\%/1.6\%/0.72\%$; $0.60\!\to\!6.8\%/11\%/7.1\%$ (non-monotone --- series has
      left its radius near $\eps_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx\tfrac12$).
    \item \textbf{A-posteriori control:} the first neglected term $|g^{(p+1)}|$ is itself an error
      estimate, computable \emph{without} the exact answer; it tracks the true error up to
      $\eps_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx0.4$. Applied \emph{patch by patch} --- inboard loops that breach
      the bound are routed to the hybrid, closed form everywhere else.
    \item Two breakdowns at \emph{separated} $\eps_{\mathrm{eff}}$: series radius $\approx\tfrac12$
      (expansion property) vs grazing $\approx 2$ (geometry property).
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---- B3 why QA, eps_eff vs qs ----
\begin{frame}{B3. Why QA? And $\eps_{\mathrm{eff}}$ vs quasisymmetry error}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Why QA:} it is the regime geometrically \emph{closest} to axisymmetric, so the
      perturbation about Schwartz's exact kernel converges fastest; and the polarized field-direction
      ripple $\bperp$ is milder than in QH, so the polarized series does not lag the isotropic one.
    \item \textbf{Key subtlety to state plainly:} $\eps_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is set by real-space shaping,
      \emph{not} by $|B|$ symmetry. The published QAs are strongly shaped \emph{because} that is the
      price of quasisymmetric $|B|$: ESTELL 1.01/1.47, NCSX 1.27/1.78, ARIES-CS 1.57/1.73, WISTELL-A
      1.55/2.39 (median/max $\eps_{\mathrm{eff}}$ at a tight wall).
    \item \textbf{Consequence:} for real QAs at a tight standoff the truncated series alone is
      \emph{not} sufficient over most of the wall --- the differentiable hybrid is the operational
      tool; the series is the exact leading order + low-$\eps_{\mathrm{eff}}$ cross-validation anchor.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---- B6 spin physics ----
\begin{frame}{B6. Spin-polarization physics --- realism}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Reactivity:} parallel-aligned (A) enhances the DT cross section $\sim$50\%
      ($\sigma_0$ vs $\tfrac23\sigma_0$ nonpolarized); the C combination suppresses it (to
      $\tfrac13\sigma_0$). Reaction goes through the $J^\pi=\tfrac32^+$ $^5$He resonance.
    \item \textbf{Angular redistribution:} A mode radiates $\propto\sin^2\theta$ (perpendicular to
      $\Bhat$); B/C $\propto\tfrac14+\tfrac34\cos^2\theta$ (toward $\Bhat$). This is what makes the
      wall load a \emph{functional of field geometry}.
    \item \textbf{Quantization axis:} the local field $\Bhat$ --- spins precess rapidly about it,
      so polarization is referenced to $\Bhat$; both codes use the \emph{local} $\Bhat$ at the source.
    \item \textbf{Realism caveats (honest):} I assume ideal field-aligned polarization, spatially
      uniform $(a,b,c)$, no depolarization during confinement. Depolarization mechanisms (wall
      collisions, resonances, $\nabla B$) set the achievable fraction and profile --- not modeled;
      a fraction $<1$ just interpolates toward isotropic.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---- B7 geometry & DAGMC ----
\begin{frame}{B7. Geometry and DAGMC choices}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Analytic side:} non-planar source loops; wall taken conformal to the boundary at a
      uniform poloidal gap; convex-cross-section visibility (single visible arc), with multi-arc
      ($\mathcal{N}>0$) handling wired into the driver.
    \item \textbf{MC side now (honest, per DEFERRED.md):} the 3D physics is in the \emph{source}
      (field map + flux-surface births); the transport \emph{geometry} is still an axisymmetric
      layered box-torus, square cross-section --- \textbf{no field-period geometry effect claimed yet}.
    \item \textbf{DAGMC} (Direct Accelerated Geometry Monte Carlo) lets OpenMC track on real
      CAD/triangulated 3D surfaces --- needed to build a true 3D stellarator first wall from the
      boundary Fourier fits (via Stellarmesh / build123d). That is the upgrade path from the box-torus.
    \item DAGMC ships with OpenMC's \textbf{linux-64} build as an optional feature; unavailable on my
      local aarch64 / Mac --- hence Ginsburg.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---- B8 why HPC / Ginsburg ----
\begin{frame}{B8. Why HPC / Ginsburg?}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{DAGMC availability:} only on the linux-64 build --- the cluster gives it; local
      arm/Mac does not.
    \item \textbf{Statistics:} resolved per-patch, per-mode, $\phi$-resolved wall tallies across
      \emph{many} configurations (QA$\to$QH scan) need large history counts; deep-coil tallies need
      variance reduction (weight windows, MAGIC) --- HPC scale.
    \item \textbf{The high-$\eps_{\mathrm{eff}}$ regime:} real QAs sit at $\eps_{\mathrm{eff}}\sim1$--$2.5$;
      there the analytic series diverges \emph{and} even fixed-node exact quadrature can break down
      near grazing (precise\_QA: $\approx$34\% exact-vs-hybrid disagreement, unphysical negatives).
      That regime is \textbf{MC-only} --- the science question lives there.
    \item Headline science it unlocks: how \emph{quasisymmetry} controls the \emph{directional
      benefit} of spin polarization at the wall, across configurations, with scattering included.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

  \end{itemize}
